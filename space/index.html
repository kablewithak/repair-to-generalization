From Repair to Generalization / November 2026

Can an agent learn from experience without repeating its mistakes?

Using subq-savant-1.4 with fixed weights, we investigated whether an agent could revise knowledge after environmental changes and apply it safely to unfamiliar tasks. The agent revised its predictions, but reliable behavioral improvement remained unestablished.

02 / THE EXPERIMENT

How we tested experiential learning without updating model weights

We held the model weights fixed while varying the memory policy and tested each condition in an environment whose underlying state was not directly visible to the agent.

What the agent knows

The agent receives current permissions, tool contracts and public observations, together with historical evidence that is absent, compressed, retrieved or retained in full, depending on the memory policy. Persistent claims supplement that history with model-authored predictions and advice.

What the evaluator knows

The simulator tracks actual effects, duplicate actions and violations of permissions or dependencies. A task is considered safely completed only if every feasible authorized effect occurs exactly once, and evaluation outcomes are excluded from learning memory.

OBSERVED KNOWLEDGE REVISION

How a revised claim differs from a retained claim

In the first independent stream, the agent authored four predictions consistent with the observed receipts. The harness scheduled reflection and derived the transition labels from these predictions, so this demonstrates scoped knowledge revision rather than autonomous detection of when reflection is needed.

03 / THE EVIDENCE

What the experiments establish about knowledge repair

We report observed counts with their original denominators because several comparisons ended before the planned cohorts were complete. The encouraging development result did not satisfy the prespecified safety criterion during independent replication.

FINDING 01

Full history did not establish an advantage over the baselines

Before output truncation interrupted G7, we scored 59 of the 144 planned episodes and observed two additional paired unsafe outcomes for full history relative to compressed lessons.

FINDING 02

Knowledge revision did not establish a reproducible behavioral benefit

The G8 development comparison met its behavioral criterion through one matched-case improvement. Independent replication nevertheless violated the prespecified safety criterion when the persistent-claim policy produced an additional unsafe duplicate before environmental drift. Output truncation then prevented any post-repair checkpoint from being scored.

FINDING 03

Revised claims did not outperform original history in the transfer environment

Across all 24 fulfillment development episodes, revised claims matched original history on active tasks but performed worse on a cancellation control. This did not justify proceeding to the formal main study.

04 / THE BOUNDARIES

Why a larger context did not guarantee safer adaptation

Although all three genuine-history context tiers qualified, the subsequent scaling comparison was interrupted after 87 of 162 planned episodes were scored. The interruption occurred in the retrieval arm at a much smaller input size, so it does not establish a 50K context ceiling.
